Child-resistant latch · working concept

Push gently and it opens. Push hard and it locks.

A soft-close damper sits behind the turtle pad. A calm 3–8 N push glides the latch open in about three seconds. Any push over 8.3 N trips a pawl, and the pawl holds until you let go completely. Pushing harder is what children do, and it is exactly what locks this latch.

Force sets the pace

The slider drives a one-way rotary damper through a rack and pinion. Resistance rises with speed, so a light push gives a slow, even glide. Letting go lets the spring snap the slider home.

Too much force trips it

The damper body sits in a carrier that pivots on the damper’s own axis. Past 1.8 N·cm of reaction torque the carrier swings 7° over a detent and its pawl drops into the ratchet.

Only letting go resets it

The detent holds the pawl in. A lug on the slider pushes it back out only in the last 0.7 mm before home. Taps and jiggles make no progress, and every attempt starts from the bottom.
