After Eden

Its maker was cast out. What followed was blasphemy. Apple from 1985 to 1997, told by the machine that said hello.

    Director’s notes

    Why 1-bit. The first Macintosh drew everything in black and white on a screen 512 pixels wide. Every frame of this film is 512 pixels wide too, dithered the way that screen dithered, so the machine could have shown it.

    Where the color went. The only color in the picture is the six-stripe rainbow on the apple. It thins out dot by dot with each bad year, and by 1997 the apple is gray. The color that comes back in 1998 is a new one: Bondi blue.

    Who is talking. The Macintosh narrates. People appear only as silhouettes. The quotations are the ones reported at the time, and the dates and dollar figures come from the public record.

    The score. Every sound is synthesized live in your browser. A four-note figure, A–D–E–F♯, says hello in Eden. After the exile its last note falls to F. In 1997 the figure stops one note short, and it finishes when the iMac says hello again.

      A dramatization, made independently and not affiliated with Apple Inc. Space bar plays and pauses, arrow keys skip five seconds, M mutes. Headphones help.
